Congress Review

Recap: EACTS and ESVS 2026

Early October brought two of our most important congresses back to back: EACTS in Barcelona for cardiac surgery and ESVS in Belgrade for vascular surgery. Here are the highlights from both.

EACTS 2026 Barcelona: Highlights

7–10 October · CCIB Barcelona · Booth #80
CARDIAC

The Medistim booth was busy throughout the 40th EACTS Annual Meeting. We had valuable conversations with new and existing contacts across the cardiothoracic community, which strengthened old relationships and started new ones.

The new Medistim App was a highlight. Many visitors stopped for a hands-on demo, and many downloaded the app on the spot. We came home with a good number of new leads, and the follow-up is already underway.

As our biggest cardiac event of the year, EACTS also gave us a clear view of where the field is heading.

The big trend this year was MIDCAB and robotic CABG. Several sessions on the topic had waiting lines, leaving some people unable to get into the room. Speakers stressed that the learning curve is steep, at 100+ cases for MIDCAB and 250–500 with robotic LITA harvest, and that keeping the same team matters.

 

The good news for us: Drs. Davierwala, Glineur and Albert are all TTFM customers, and they called TTFM key to quality assurance in MIDCAB and robotics.

Robotic CABG is still only 1.8% of STS cases, but it’s growing, and that’s an opportunity for us.

Dr. John Puskas also argued for intraoperative graft assessment with TTFM and imaging during a session on improving CABG outcomes in the OR. Later in the day, he honored David Taggart by discussing his ART Trial.

Thank you to everyone who stopped by our booth. See you next year!

ESVS 2026: A Successful Congress in Belgrade

5–9 October · Belgrade
VASCULAR

ESVS 2026, held in Belgrade from 5–9 October, was a great success for Medistim! We had a very productive exhibition with many valuable leads and excellent interactions with vascular surgeons from across Europe.

 

We took part in four hands-on workshops, each attended by 20 vascular surgeons. Every participant got to experience Medistim’s Completion Control solutions in training on Peripheral Bypass, AV Access and Carotid Endarterectomy (CEA).

We also took part in a new workshop on Acute Mesenteric Ischemia, led by Dr. Vikatmaa, and hosted a PATENT investigators’ meeting, which made room for valuable discussion and collaboration.

A big thank you to Kamilla from the UK, Carsten from Denmark and especially Goran from our local distributor, BormiaMed, for their excellent support throughout the congress!

Overall, a highly successful event with strong engagement, valuable connections and great opportunities to further develop our vascular business.

Publication Highlight

Proximal ligation in coronary artery bypass grafting for anomalous origin of right coronary artery: A case series with transit-time flow measurement

Masaki N, Saito D, Tabayashi A, Shioya M, Edaki D, Koizumi J, Kin H (2026)

Department of Cardiovascular Surgery, Iwate Medical University, Japan

Asian Cardiovasc Thorac Ann. 2026;34(5):465–469. doi: 10.1177/02184923261446691

Keywords: anomalous aortic origin of the right coronary artery, coronary artery bypass grafting, flow competition, proximal ligation, systolic reverse flow, transit-time flow measurement

Anomalous aortic origin of the right coronary artery (AAoRCA) is a rare anomaly linked to sudden cardiac death, particularly when the artery runs between the aorta and the pulmonary artery. CABG is one way to treat it, but there is a well-known risk: blood still flowing through the native right coronary artery competes with the graft and can cause it to occlude.

The team at Iwate Medical University used Medistim TTFM in all seven cases in this series. The measurements frequently showed systolic reverse flow in the graft, a sign of competitive flow. After the proximal right coronary artery was ligated, the reverse flow disappeared and mean graft flow increased. Long-term outcomes were excellent, and the authors conclude that CABG is a suitable treatment for AAoRCA when proximal ligation is done appropriately.

This is a strong example of TTFM going beyond graft verification: here it helped the surgeons see competitive flow, decide on ligation, and confirm the result on the table, before closing the chest.

Intraoperative transit-time flow measurement in patient no. 7. (A) Marked systolic reverse flow was observed before proximal right coronary artery ligation. (B) After ligation, the systolic reverse flow resolved, and mean graft flow increased.
