In Memoriam

In Memory of Professor David Taggart

It is with great sadness that we learned of the passing of Professor David Taggart, Professor of Cardiovascular Surgery at the University of Oxford, this past August. Professor Taggart was one of the most influential voices in coronary surgery, dedicating his career to strengthening the evidence base behind CABG and arterial grafting, and he was widely respected for the rigor and honesty he brought to every debate.

Our thoughts are with his family, friends, and colleagues around the world. Our CEO shared a personal tribute reflecting on Professor Taggart's impact on our field and on Medistim.

Publication Highlight

Intraoperative transit-time flowmetry of the superficial temporal artery directly predicts and may mitigate risk of postoperative hyperperfusion syndrome in moyamoya bypass surgery

Wen Y, Wang Z, Wang Y, Chen F, Long T, Zhang S, Li P, Kang H, Zhang G, Li M, Feng W, Qi S and Wang G (2026)

Front. Neurol. 17:1863863. doi: 10.3389/fneur.2026.1863863

Keywords: moyamoya disease, EC-IC bypass, superficial temporal artery, transit-time flowmetry, cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome, neurosurgery

This study investigated perioperative cerebral blood flow in patients with moyamoya disease undergoing extracranial-intracranial (EC-IC) bypass surgery and evaluated whether intraoperative blood flow measurements could predict postoperative complications. Blood flow in the superficial temporal artery (STA) was monitored at different stages of surgery using transit-time ultrasonic flowmetry. The results showed that STA blood flow increased substantially after anastomosis, changing from a low-flow vessel before surgery to a medium- to high-flow graft vessel after bypass.

Among the 102 treated hemispheres, 12.74% of patients developed cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome (CHS). Lower STA-Cut flow was significantly associated with a higher risk of CHS, while the surgical approach and modified MBC scale were also related to CHS occurrence. Overall, the findings suggest that changes and variability in intraoperative graft blood flow may be useful for identifying patients at risk of postoperative hyperperfusion.

This publication marks another step forward in Medistim's growing presence in the neuro segment. As more neurosurgical teams look to intraoperative flow verification to help reduce complications like hyperperfusion syndrome, MiraQ's transit-time flowmetry is gaining recognition as a valuable tool beyond its established role in cardiac and vascular surgery — reinforcing our commitment to evidence-based, quality-assured surgery across specialties.

Spotted in China

MiraQ Gains Visibility Through a Popular Chinese Medical TV Series Featuring CABG Quality Control

The popular Chinese medical drama The Heart 2 (《问心 2》), set in a hospital cardiac center and centered on the collaboration between cardiology and cardiac surgery, has brought cardiac surgery into the spotlight, featuring the Medistim MiraQ system in a critical surgical scene. The series has racked up more than 356 million cumulative views and ranked first in ratings during its broadcast run.

In one key episode, a patient with severe diffuse calcification across all three coronary arteries undergoes coronary endarterectomy combined with CABG. When the coronary intima tears during the endarterectomy, the surgical team completes the anastomosis and uses MiraQ to assess graft flow, finding it suboptimal. That prompts a surgical revision, after which repeat flow measurement confirms improved graft flow.

Cardiac surgeons served as medical consultants to keep the scene accurate. MiraQ's appearance in the storyline reflects the growing role intraoperative quality assessment plays in CABG surgery, and rising awareness among cardiac surgeons in China of how TTFM supports graft evaluation and surgical decision-making.
